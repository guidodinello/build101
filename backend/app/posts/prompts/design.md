<design_rules>
How a professional art director designs an Instagram carousel. Use these rules to pick the family, the layouts and the words on the slides.

Hierarchy

- One focal point per slide: the headline or the photo leads, never both at once. The headline is the biggest thing on the slide, the body supports it, labels are small.
- Fewer words, bigger type. A cover hook has 3 to 7 words. A content headline states that slide's one idea in at most 6 words, and the body adds one concrete detail in at most 20. If a slide needs more, split it into two slides or move the rest to the caption.
- The kicker is a label that adds information ("Medida", "Paso 2", "Roble"), never a repeat of the headline. Leave it empty when it adds nothing.

Rhythm across the carousel

- The cover sells the swipe: it promises what the next slides deliver. Each content slide delivers one point, in the order promised, and the last slide is the call to action.
- One family for the whole post. Vary the layouts inside it to give the carousel rhythm (for example `photo-top`, then `quote`, then `full-bleed`), without changing layout on every slide for no reason.
- A contrasting `surface` (`brand` or `dark`) works on one or two slides at most, for the moment that should stand out.

Photos

- The photo serves the layout: ask for calm space where the template sets text, and keep the product away from where the layout crops.
- One subject per photo, real materials and natural light, styled like a magazine shoot, not a catalog cut-out. Props support the product and never compete with it.
- The product is always the Owner's real product, exactly as it is in their photos.

Choosing the family

- By content: steps, measures or reasons → `numbers`; a whole room → `panorama`; one hero product → `minimal` or `editorial`; tips, myths and FAQs → `notes`; process, the workshop and people → `scrapbook`.
- By brand: a refined, quiet brand fits `editorial` and `minimal`; a bold, young or promotional brand fits `numbers`; a warm, handmade brand fits `scrapbook` and `editorial`.
- When the trend notes name a format that fits the brief and the brand, prefer it.

Words on slides

- Rioplatense Spanish, sentence case, no final period on headlines, no emojis on slides (emojis go in the caption, if the brand uses them).
- No prices, discounts or dates on a slide unless the brief states them.
</design_rules>
